Interior Cladding

Design draft Updated 2026-07-24
On this page
  • Overview
  • Species / grade strategy
  • Installation notes
  • Open items

Overview #

The wood the bather sees and touches. It must tolerate heat and moisture, resist warping, and stay comfortable to touch — which rules out high-resin, high-density, or heat-conductive materials.

Decision (mixed species):

  • Western Hemlock for the walls, ceiling, and floor duckboard — the majority of the interior. Mid-priced, neutral-scent, clean look; a common sauna wood.
  • Western Red Cedar (clear VG) for the benches only — the wood people sit on, where low conductivity + no sap/knots matter most.
  • Exterior siding is cheap non-cedar (T1-11 / LP SmartSide / vinyl).

Pricing in ../01-research/cedar-pricing.md and ../02-planning/cost-options.md.

Species / grade strategy #

Use Species / grade Why
Benches (skin contact) Western Red Cedar, clear VG No knots/sap, stable, comfortable, won’t burn
Walls / ceiling Western Hemlock T&G Majority of interior; mid-cost, neutral scent
Floor duckboard Western Hemlock Matches walls (see note on wet-floor oiling)
Exterior siding Non-cedar (T1-11 / LP / vinyl) Cheapest; see ../02-planning/cost-options.md

Note (hemlock care): hemlock has little scent and benefits from oiling — left dry it can crack over time (per the handbook). Oil it and let the room dry between uses. For the wet duckboard, oil thoroughly, or keep that one piece in cedar for extra rot resistance if you prefer.

Installation notes #

  • Install over battens to preserve the air gap behind the foil barrier.
  • Leave expansion room; fasten to hide/limit hot metal fastener heads.
  • Use stainless fasteners — cedar tannins (and damp) corrode/stain regular steel.
  • Finish: bare or a sauna-specific paraffin oil — never standard varnish. Oil the hemlock to keep it from drying/cracking.
  • Floor: the wet floor surface stays waterproof (membrane/tile); the hemlock shows as the removable duckboard you stand on, not the sealed floor itself.
