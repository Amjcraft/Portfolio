Overview #

The structural skeleton: base/floor, walls, and roof. Stud depth must accommodate the chosen insulation; the roof must shed the site’s snow/rain load. Values below feed the takeoff in 02-planning/materials-and-tools.md.

Structure summary #

  • Footprint: ~9.5 × 8 ft exterior. Front/back walls at ~9.5 ft; the extra ~6″ vs. the earlier 9 ft compensates for the deeper 2×6 walls so the door still fits beside the heater. Interior ~8.2 × 6.7 ft.
  • Walls: 2×6 framing @ 16″ OC (upgraded from 2×4 for the cold Bend climate and the ≥8×8 size — per the Sauna Construction Handbook). Low wall 8 ft, high wall ~9.5 ft.
  • Roof: shed / mono-pitch, sloping side-to-side (across the ~9.5 ft width, as you face the front door) — high on one side wall, low on the other.

Note (roof is under an existing building’s roof): the sauna sits beneath an overhead structure, so its own roof carries essentially no snow or direct rain load — only self-weight. The shallow slope is just to shed blow-in/drips, and 2×6 rafters @ 16″ OC over the ~9.5 ft span are adequate. If the covering structure were ever removed, re-check rafter sizing and add a proper low-slope weather roof.

Base / floor #

Raised wood floor on the existing slab (shed-style). No concrete work; the platform cavity carries the drain out to a French drain. Full detail in foundation.md.

  • PT 2×6 joists @ 16″ OC on the slab → ~5.5″ cavity for the drain run.
  • Capillary break between PT and concrete; ¾″ ext/PT plywood subfloor.
  • Waterproof finish floor pitched to a floor drain; removable duckboard on top.
  • Drain: 2″ PVC, sloped ≥¼″/ft, through the cavity → out → French drain.
  • Walls bear on the platform’s PT rim/sill; platform anchored to the slab as needed.

Walls #

All four walls: 2×6 studs @ 16″ OC, single bottom plate + double top plate, corrosion-resistant fasteners throughout.

Roof pitch (governs all rake-wall cuts) #
Rise 9.5 ft − 8 ft = 1.5 ft = 18″
Run side wall to side wall = 9.5 ft = 114″
Pitch 18/114 per 12″ ≈ 2:12
Bevel angle arctan(18/114) ≈ 9°

The two rake walls (front and back) have top plates ripped at 9° along their top edge, and every stud in those walls gets a matching 9° bevel cut at the top.

Front wall — rake (114″ wide, 96″ low / 114″ high, has door) #

Every stud is a different length — heights increase from right (low) to left (high). The top plate follows the roof slope and is ripped at 9° along its top edge before raising.

The door rough opening (~28″ wide × 76″ tall) falls in the right-center zone, between the bench area and the heater corner. Target: center the RO at roughly 70–78″ from the high (left) end — leaves ~28–36″ at the right for the heater corner. Confirm on site against bench leg placement and heater clearances before cutting.

Members for the front wall:

  • 9 full-height rake studs (includes 2 king studs flanking the RO); 9° bevel top cut
  • 2 jack (trimmer) studs — cut to 74½" (= 76″ RO − 1½" for bottom plate)
  • 2–3 cripple studs above the header — same 9° bevel, lengths measured on site; cut from offcuts
  • Double 2×6 header — see Rough Openings below
  • Bottom plate: 1× 2×6 @ 114″ (cut from 10′); use PT
  • Top plates (×2): 2× 2×6 @ 114″ (10′); rip a 9° bevel along the top edge of each

Back wall — rake (114″ wide, solid) #

Same geometry as the front wall but no openings. All studs are full-height rake cuts.

  • 9 studs (2 end + 7 field); varying 91½"–109½"; 9° bevel top cut on all
  • Bottom plate: 1× 2×6 @ 114″ (10′); use PT
  • Top plates (×2): 2× 2×6 @ 114″ (10′); 9° bevel ripped along top edge

Corner framing #

At each of the 4 corners add 1 corner nailer stud — a full-height stud set perpendicular to and flush with the corner, giving the intersecting wall a nailing surface on the interior side.

  • 4 corners × 1 nailer = 4 extra studs from 10′ stock (match the corner wall height)
  • Face-nail the nailer to the pair of corner studs; then nail the intersecting wall plate into it

Cap plate corner overlap #

The outer (cap) top plate of one wall laps the corner to tie the two walls together. Standard practice: let the front/back cap plates run full length (114″); trim the side wall cap plates to butt tightly. Nail through the lap to complete the box. Work this out at each corner during erection.

Roof #

  • Shed rafters (2×6 @ 16″ OC) spanning the ~9.5 ft width, bearing on the high and low side walls; front/back walls get the sloped (rake) top plates described above.
  • Roof sheathing + underlayment; a simple membrane cap is enough under the existing overhead structure (no full weather roof / snow detailing required here).
  • Modest overhang (~6–12″) on the side walls to protect the walls below.

Door framing detail #

A double 2×6 header is more than adequate for a 28″ span. In the rake wall, the cripple studs above the header also get the 9° bevel.

Confirm the RO position against the heater spec sheet (minimum clearance to combustibles) and the lower bench leg location before cutting the bottom plate.

All hardware must be hot-dip galvanized (HDG) or stainless — heat + humidity cycling will destroy bare steel in a few seasons.
