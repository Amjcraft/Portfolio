Insulation & Vapor Barrier

Design draft Updated 2026-07-24
On this page

  • Assembly (inside → outside)
  • Ceiling
  • Key details
  • Open items
  • Sources

Overview #

The wall/ceiling assembly determines whether the sauna heats efficiently and survives repeated heat-and-moisture cycles without rot. This is where an outdoor electric sauna lives or dies.

Assembly (inside → outside) #

  1. Interior cladding — see interior-cladding.md.
  2. Air gap — thin ventilated gap behind cladding (battens) so the wood can dry.
  3. Foil vapor barrier — reflective, foil-faced; all seams taped with foil tape, overlapped, sealed at penetrations. Reflects radiant heat back and blocks moisture.
  4. Insulation — mineral wool (rock/stone wool) in the 5.5″ 2×6 stud bays → R-23. Non-combustible, handles heat, doesn’t off-gas like some foams.
  5. Structural / sheathing — framing per framing.md.
  6. Weather-resistive barrier + exterior siding — outdoor envelope.

Decision: Use mineral wool, not fiberglass or foam, for heat tolerance and safety. Walls are 2×6 → R-23 (upgraded from 2×4/R-15): the cold Bend climate warrants it, and the extra insulation lets the circuit-limited 9.5 kW heater keep up with the ~11 m³ room (the handbook’s “add insulation if the heater is small” advice).

Ceiling #

  • Insulate the ceiling at least as well as the walls — most heat collects up top.
  • Continue the foil barrier across the ceiling, sealed to the walls.

Key details #

  • Overlap and tape every foil seam; the barrier must be continuous.
  • Seal around the heater cable penetration and vents.
  • Keep the air gap so cladding can dry between uses.
