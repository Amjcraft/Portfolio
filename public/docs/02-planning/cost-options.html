Overview #

Ways to bring the all-cedar estimate down without hurting the sauna. The current all-WRC build runs ~$9,450–$20,150 all-in (see budget.html). The savings live in three places — wall/ceiling cladding, siding, and bench wood — not the floor.

Reality check on tile: flooring is only ~$150–430 of the whole project. Tile is a durable, good-looking choice, but it’s mid-priced and still needs waterproofing under it — so it won’t meaningfully cut cost. Pick it because you like it, not to save money. The cheapest wet floor is a sealed membrane or vinyl + a duckboard.

Decisions made so far #

  • Walls = 2×6 / R-23 (upgraded from 2×4 for the cold Bend climate; ~cost-neutral).
  • Interior wood = Western Hemlock for walls, ceiling, and floor duckboard.
  • Bench slats = Western Red Cedar (clear VG) — the one cedar splurge.
  • Bench frame = hem-fir 2×4 (hidden; matches interior, priced like SPF).
  • Exterior siding = cheap, non-cedar (T1-11 / LP SmartSide / vinyl — see Lever 2).
  • Door = DIY with an enlarged glass lite (~$180–420 materials vs. $400–900 prehung).
  • Rear window removed.

With those applied, the estimate is now ~$7,950–$15,750 all-in (budget.html). Choosing hemlock over cedar for the majority of the interior is what pulled the high end down (~$10/ft² vs. cedar’s ~$16). Remaining swing is hemlock grade/quality + siding choice.

Lever 1 — Interior wood species (biggest saving) #

Cedar is prized for aroma + rot resistance, but it’s the priciest option. Other purpose-cut sauna woods perform as well or better and cost far less.

Detailed per-species pricing: [[cedar-pricing]], [[hemlock-pricing]], [[aspen-pricing]] (in ../01-research/).

Wood (T&G interior) Material $/ft² Durability PNW notes
Knotty pine/spruce $3–5 ok Cheapest, but resinous — keep off hot zones/benches
Western Hemlock ⭐ $5–10 10–20 yr Best budget value — domestic PNW, durable, neutral; oil it
Aspen (economy) $4–8 low Palest, coolest to touch, allergen-free; soft, needs good drying
Aspen (imported sauna-grade) $10–13 low Same wood, import premium — not always cheaper than hemlock
Basswood $4–8 low Like aspen; scent-neutral
Knotty WRC $4.5–6 15–20 yr Cheapest cedar; some aroma; knots can bleed sap near heater
Thermo-aspen/spruce $7–12 good Heat-treated, stable, darker
Clear VG WRC $12–20 15–20 yr Premium aroma + stability

Best budget-friendly pick (PNW): Western Hemlock. It’s domestic (cheap to source in Bend/Portland), durable (10–20 yr), and neutral-looking. Aspen is the most comfortable wood and cheap in economy grade, but it’s soft, less durable, and often imported — so in the PNW it’s not reliably cheaper than hemlock. Knotty pine is cheaper still but resinous. **This is why the current plan uses hemlock walls/ceiling

  • a little clear cedar on the benches** — the value-optimal mix.

Key insight: benches don’t need cedar — aspen/basswood are actually better against skin (lower conductivity, no sap, no splinters). Swapping bench slats from clear VG cedar to aspen saves ~$300–550 and improves comfort.

Lever 4 — Sheathing / breathability (handbook flag #3, priced separately) #

The handbook cautions that plain OSB isn’t vapor-permeable, so the wall struggles to dry outward (moisture risk over years). Our base BOM uses OSB + house wrap. Below is the cost of switching to a more permeable air/water barrier — not in the main total; add whichever you choose.

Compares the wall+roof sheathing + wall WRB only (~394 ft²); roofing underlayment is common to all.

Approach Cost Δ vs. base Permeable? Notes
OSB + house wrap (base) $235–434 — ✗ poor outward drying Cheapest solid nailing surface
Plywood + Tyvek $430–694 +$195–260 ~ better Plywood breathes more than OSB
Zip System ⭐ $544–822 +$310–390 ✓ (facer ~12 perm) Integrated WRB, taped seams; clean & durable
Tyvek + let-in bracing (no sheet) $100–190 −$135–245 ✓ Cheapest & breathes, but no solid nail base, more skill

Recommendation: if you want to honor the handbook without much fuss, Zip System is the clean choice (+$310–390) — it’s the WRB and the bracing in one taped panel. If budget is tight, plywood + Tyvek is a cheaper permeable step up from OSB. The bracing-only route is cheapest and breathes best but is the most finicky to build.

Three packages #

All-in (shell + interior + deferred electrical/heater), rough PNW 2026:

Package What changes All-in
Premium (current) Clear/knotty WRC everywhere, WRC siding, glass door ~$10,500–$13,500
Balanced ⭐ recommended Aspen walls/ceiling + benches; LP SmartSide siding; optional WRC accent wall for aroma; tile or membrane floor ~$9,000–$11,000
Budget Aspen/knotty-pine interior; T1-11 siding; membrane/vinyl floor; simpler wood door ~$7,500–$9,000

Approx. shell+interior (before deferred electrical/heater):

Package Shell + interior
Premium ~$7,800–$17,000
Balanced ~$6,200–$12,200
Budget ~$5,250–$10,300

Don’t cheap out on: insulation + foil vapor barrier ($600–895) — it’s what makes the sauna heat efficiently and not rot. Keep it regardless of package.

Recommendation #

Balanced package. Use aspen for the walls, ceiling, and benches — it’s roughly half the cost of clear cedar, scent-neutral, and better against skin. Put the money you’d have spent on cedar into a small cedar accent (one feature wall or the door surround) if you want the aroma, and use LP SmartSide siding for durability at a lower price. That trims roughly $1,500–3,000+ off the all-cedar build while keeping a first-class sauna.
