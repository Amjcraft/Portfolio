Western Hemlock Pricing (PNW)

✅ SELECTED — the primary interior wood: walls, ceiling, and floor duckboard (T&G), plus the bench frame (hem-fir 2×4, hemlock family). Cedar is used only for the bench slats. See ../02-planning/materials-and-tools.html.

Overview #

Rough 2026 Western Hemlock pricing for interior sauna paneling/cladding, for the Bend, OR / Portland (PNW) market. Hemlock is a domestic PNW species, so it’s readily available and priced well locally — our chosen wood for the walls, ceiling, and duckboard. Prices are materials only; confirm with local suppliers/mills.

Character & grade #

  • Light, uniform tone, little/no scent (unlike cedar), clean modern look.
  • No natural oils like cedar → it needs good ventilation + occasional oiling, or it can dry, gray, or check (surface-crack) over years.
  • Sold as clear/knot-free sauna T&G (usual) or construction-grade boards.
  • Lifespan ~10–20 years in a well-ventilated sauna.

Pricing found (2026, materials only) #

Product Price Per ft² (approx.)
Hemlock sauna T&G, ½″ × 3½″ (Superior Saunas) $9.99 / ft² ~$10 / ft²
Hemlock sauna T&G (general range) — ~$5–10 / ft²
Nordic spruce T&G (for context) $3–5 / bd-ft —

Planning range used #

  • Interior T&G (walls + ceiling): $5–10 / ft² — cheaper at a local PNW mill, toward $10 for finished sauna-grade from a specialty supplier.
  • Bench frame — hem-fir 2×4 construction lumber: ~$4–8 / 8-ft board. “Hem-fir” is a standard, economical framing grade (hemlock/true-fir species group) priced right alongside SPF — so using it for the hidden bench frame adds no real premium while matching the interior species. (Distinct from the finished T&G paneling above.)

Sourcing (Bend / PNW) #

  • Hemlock grows in the PNW — local lumberyards/mills in Bend or near Portland should stock it or mill T&G to order, usually cheaper than specialty sauna sellers.
  • Buy kiln-dried, straight stock; hand-pick for minimal knots on visible faces.

Pros / cons vs. cedar & aspen #

  • vs. cedar: ~30–50% cheaper, no aroma, lighter color; less naturally rot-resistant (needs the vented/oiled care above).
  • vs. aspen: similar or slightly cheaper (hemlock is domestic; aspen is often imported), and more durable/harder than soft aspen.

Verdict: For a PNW budget build, hemlock is the value sweet spot — domestic, available, durable enough, and much cheaper than cedar. It’s our walls/ceiling choice.
