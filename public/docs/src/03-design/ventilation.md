## Overview

Good airflow keeps the heat even, brings in fresh air for bathers, and lets the
room dry out afterward (preventing mold and prolonging the wood). Even with an
electric heater — no combustion exhaust needed — ventilation is essential.

## Typical electric-sauna scheme

- **Air intake:** low, near/beside the heater, so incoming air is drawn up over
  the stones and heated.
- **Air exhaust:** on the opposite wall, higher up (but often below the top bench),
  adjustable, to pull air across the room.
- **Drying vent:** ability to open things up after use so the room dries.

> **Note:** Exact vent placement depends on the heater manual and layout. Capture
> the chosen scheme here with a sketch in `docs/assets/images/`.

## Mechanical vs. gravity

- **Gravity (natural):** simplest; relies on intake/exhaust placement and stack effect.
- **Mechanical:** a small exhaust fan for more consistent turnover; more complex.

Decide based on how airtight the outdoor build ends up and comfort goals.
