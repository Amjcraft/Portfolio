## Overview

Rough 2026 Aspen pricing for interior sauna paneling/cladding, Bend/Portland (PNW)
market. Aspen is the classic **scent-neutral, allergen-free** sauna wood — very
comfortable against skin — but it's **soft** and often **imported (European)**, which
can push its price up to or past domestic hemlock. Materials only; confirm locally.

## Character & grade

- **Pale, nearly white**, **no resin/sap, no scent**, **hypoallergenic** — won't
  splinter or off-gas; **stays cool to the touch** (great for benches/backrests).
- **Soft and the least rot-resistant** of cedar/hemlock/aspen — needs **good
  ventilation + drying**, or it can gray, mold, or check within a few years.
- Cheap and easy to **replace** board-by-board if a piece degrades.

## Planning range used

- **Interior T&G: $5–10 / ft²** (economy domestic low; imported sauna-grade high).

## Sourcing (Bend / PNW)

- Less locally grown than hemlock/cedar; often ordered through specialty sauna
  suppliers (imported) or a hardwood dealer — **get quotes**, it varies a lot.
- Best reserved for **where you touch it** (benches, backrests) to get the most from
  its cool, splinter-free surface.

## Pros / cons vs. cedar & hemlock

- **vs. cedar:** much cheaper (economy grade), no aroma, palest look; far less durable.
- **vs. hemlock:** softer and less rot-resistant; **similar or higher price** in the
  PNW (import premium). Wins on comfort/appearance, loses on durability and (often) cost.

> **Verdict:** Aspen is the **most comfortable** interior wood and fine for budget
> walls *if* ventilation is good — but in the PNW it's **not reliably cheaper than
> hemlock**, and it's softer. Best used as an **accent or bench/backrest**, not
> necessarily the whole interior.
